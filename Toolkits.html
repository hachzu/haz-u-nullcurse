How To Use The Curse Tracker

What This Does

The curse tracker is the sidebar on the left plus the four pools further down the page. It reads your current level, player count, game mode, difficulty, and whichever enemies you have marked active, and shows you exactly which curses are actually available to pick in a real Nullscape run at that combination of settings. Every curse you click gets added to the Active Curses list in the sidebar, and the sidebar keeps a running Medal Payout total based on whichever medal curses you currently have selected.

How To Use It

  1. CURRENT LEVEL
    15
    1Set your level
    Type your current in-run level into the CURRENT LEVEL box at the top of the sidebar. Nearly everything else on this page checks against that number, so it is worth setting first. Curses and enemies each have their own unlock level, and the pools below will only show what you actually qualify for unless you turn on Show All Curses (Greater Curses are the one exception - more on that further down).
  2. Solo
    Duo
    2Set your player count and game mode
    PLAYER COUNT and the GAME MODE buttons (Solo, Duo, Party, Party+) affect which curses are allowed. A couple of curses, like Mart Infection and Deadly Melody, are turned off entirely in Solo. Your player count also feeds into the Medal Payout math and the Shop Upgrade Calculator's prices, so it is worth keeping accurate even if you never touch a curse.
  3. Casual
    Extreme
    3Pick a difficulty
    Standard and Extreme show you the exact same curse pool - Extreme only changes prices over in the Shop Upgrade Calculator, it does not touch curses at all. Casual is the one that matters here: a handful of curses (things like Bigger Tripmines, More Tripmines, Minefield, Barotrauma, and the greater curse Run) are disabled outright on Casual and will not appear no matter what. Switching difficulty never removes a curse you already picked under a different one, it only changes what you are able to add going forward.
  4. Bell
    4Mark your active enemies
    Down in ACTIVE ENEMIES, click whichever enemies are currently active in your run. This unlocks every curse tied to that enemy. A few enemies show a small badge in their own corner because they need another enemy active first - Voidbound Baby needs two Babys, Voidbound Guardian needs a Guardian - and Baby and Husk specifically can be clicked again after that to stack up to two active at once (some curses, like Husk Express, actually require that second stack).
  5. Fake Count
    Fake Count
    5Browse the pools and click to add a curse
    Scroll down to see four pools: Medal Curses at the very top for anything that pays out a bonus, then Global Curses, Enemy Curses, and Greater Curses. Under the default settings, only curses you actually qualify for right now appear at all - anything you are missing a level, an enemy, or a curse for simply is not in the list yet. Click a card once to add that curse to your run; clicking it again does nothing unless the curse allows more than one stack (shown as a small "x / max" readout on the card).
  6. Random Spawn
    6Manage what you picked
    Everything you add lands in the ACTIVE CURSES list in the sidebar, with Greater Curses listed first, medal curses second, and everything else after that. Use the search box there to filter that list down by name (it only filters what you have already added, it does not touch the pools below), click the small × on an entry to remove just that one, use RESET RUN to wipe your level, difficulty, enemies, and curses back to a fresh run, or use REMOVE ALL CURSES to clear only the curses while leaving your level, mode, and enemies as they are.

Greater Curses Work A Little Differently

The other three pools hide a curse completely the moment you fall short of any requirement. Greater Curses do not follow that rule for level: as long as an enemy-type Greater Curse's required enemy is active (and nothing else about it is blocked, like an exclusive-group conflict), it always shows up in the pool, even far below the level it unlocks at - it will just sit there dimmed with a level badge until you actually reach it, instead of disappearing and reappearing later. This is intentional: it lets you see and plan around Greater Curses well ahead of time without needing to flip on Show All Curses just for that one pool.

Mutually Exclusive Curses

A handful of curse pairs can never both be active in a real run, and the tracker enforces that. Examples include High Roller / Tweaked Odds, Lap 2 / Fragile Gifts, Mart Infection / Mart Slide, Closer Husk / Further Husk, and Ballet of Blades / Blade Bombardment. Picking one side normally makes the other side disappear from its pool entirely, the same as any other unmet requirement.

With Show All Curses on, the blocked side still renders so you can see it exists, dimmed like anything else you cannot currently pick - but it will not carry a lock badge explaining why, since the reason is not a level, an enemy, or another curse. A dimmed card with no badge at all almost always means it is either exclusive-locked by something you already picked, or disabled for your current game mode.

With Unlock All Curses on, this exclusivity check is skipped entirely (this is the one dependency Unlock All does not enforce), so you can actually select both sides of a pair at once for planning purposes, even though that would never be possible in an actual run.

Show All Curses, Unlock All Curses, and Hide Medal Curses

Three switches sit above the pools and control how much you see. Show All Curses reveals every curse in every pool, even ones you do not currently qualify for, so you can plan several levels ahead instead of only seeing what is available right now.

Unlock All Curses only does something once Show All Curses is switched on, and stays disabled until then. It lets you actually click and select those normally locked curses with no level, enemy, curse, or exclusive-group requirement enforced. It never overrides a curse's own hard cap though: a curse already at its own maximum stack still cannot be added again, and a curse disabled for your current game mode (like Mart Infection in Solo) still cannot be selected either.

Hide Medal Curses hides the dedicated Medal Curses section at the very top of the page. Curses that pay out a medal do not disappear when you do this, they just show up in their normal Global Curses or Enemy Curses section instead, still highlighted and still carrying their reward badge, so you never lose track of which ones are worth a bonus.

Medal Curses And Medal Payout

Curses marked with the cyan and pink highlight pay out a medal bonus while they are active. The MEDAL PAYOUT number in the sidebar, just under the difficulty buttons, adds together a base amount plus the value of every medal curse you currently have selected, scaled up by your player count. Each medal curse's own reward badge shows the specific slice of that total it is responsible for.

If you would rather keep a medal curse's payout without living with its downside, open it in the Active Curses list and press Purify. Purifying a curse marks its entry gray and drops its actual effect out of your run, while still counting toward your Medal Payout. The tracker also shows the Golden Gift price you would pay to purify it in an actual run. Press Purify again on a purified entry to undo it and go back to the curse being fully active - just note that if you had stacked the curse more than once before purifying it, un-purifying brings it back at a single stack, not the higher count it had before.
